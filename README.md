# EDUBRIDGE-CAPSTONE-PROJECT
THIS IS A REPOSITORY OF MY CAPSTONE PROJECT AT EDUBRIDGE

SQL QUESTIONS FROM ADVENTUREWORKS DATABASE.

1.Top Sales Representatives by YTD Sales & Territory: Pull a clean list of all sales reps—showing their full names, job titles, and year-to-date sales—alongside their assigned territory names and regions, sorted from highest to lowest YTD sales.

2. Customers with No Orders (Potential Churn): Track down all individual customers who have never placed an order, pulling their customer IDs, names, and email addresses to see who is slipping through the cracks.

3. Products Never Ordered: Run a check on the catalog to find every product that has never appeared on a sales order detail line, retrieving their product IDs, names, and product numbers.

4. High-Value Orders with Customer Details: Pull every sales order where the total due crossed $50,000, displaying the order IDs, dates, final totals, and the customer's full name.

5. Product Category Sales Breakdown: Break down where revenue is actually coming from by calculating the total quantity sold and total gross revenue for each product category, ranked from top to bottom.

6. Employees with Multiple Department History: Look at internal mobility by finding employees who have moved across more than one department historically, showing their IDs, names, and the count of distinct departments they have worked in.

7. Products Priced Above Category Average: Using a correlated sub-query, spot all products whose list prices sit strictly higher than the average price of other items in their specific subcategory.

8. Top Customers by Lifetime Purchase Value: Identify the most valuable buyers by ranking the top 10 customers based on their total lifetime spend, complete with their names and totals.

9. Vendors Supplying High-Cost Components: Audit the supply chain by pulling a list of all vendors supplying components with a standard cost over $500, including the vendor name, product name, and cost.

10. Employees with Pay Rates Above Department Average: Check internal pay equity by identifying any employees whose current pay rate sits higher than the average pay rate for their specific department.

11. Monthly Sales Growth Trend: Map out the revenue trajectory over time by calculating total monthly sales broken down by year and month number.

12. Products with Low Inventory Relative to Sales: Flag potential stock issues by finding products where total inventory across all locations sits below 50% of the total quantity historically sold.

13. Sales Persons Exceeding Regional Average: Isolate the top performers by listing sales reps whose total YTD sales beat the average YTD sales of everyone else working in their specific territory.

14. Product Co-Occurrence (Cross-Selling Analysis): Dig into buying habits by finding pairs of products that frequently get bought together on the exact same sales order, counting how often that co-occurrence happens.

15. Customers Who Purchased Every Product in a Specific Subcategory: Find the ultimate power users by pulling a list of customers who have bought every single product belonging to the "Mountain Bikes" subcategory.
